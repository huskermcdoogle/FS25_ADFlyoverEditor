# Fit methods and the grade

*Draft 2, 2026-09-30. Working notes for us.*

The clear must place a centre and stubs that suit the junction, then check its own work before it
deletes anything. This page says how: name the junction's **situation**, pick a **fit** that suits it,
**grade** the fit in game, and ask you only when no fit passes. It builds on
[What the tool can read](03-data-sources.md). Code, the source of every number and research files are in
[Fit methods: reference](04b-fit-methods-reference.md).

Numbers are for Riverbend Springs and our test save, by card number. **Proposal** marks an idea not
built. **Quick trial** means tried offline in throwaway code. Nothing on this page has run in game.
Paint numbers count asphalt only, the edge we decided on.

Words used below:

- **The box:** where the two roads' paving overlaps, as the clear works it out from the lanes. Each road
  gets a strip around its middle line. The clear cuts each lane where it enters the other road's strip.
  The box's centre is the clear's centre today.
- **The meeting point:** where the tool says the side road meets the main road's middle line. It names
  the kind and the angle. The numbers here come from the centreline experiment, which is not
  committed. The hybrid you chose as "B" (side road read nearer its own start) is not built yet.
  Usually 0.4 m from the
  box's centre, but 2-6 m away at 2, 26, 27 and 66.
- **A fit:** a centre, each road's middle line near the junction, and where each stub ends.
- **Arrow board:** the two-way sign opposite a side road. A second opinion, good to about 2 m. Only this
  map and Alma have the signs.

## The idea

1. **Name the situation** from the lanes as they are, before any fit, and keep that name (proposal;
   Still open 6).
2. **Fit it** with the method that suits that situation. A hard case can get two or three fits.
3. **Grade each fit** against the paint and the lanes. Same pass mark on every map. Signs and map groups
   only add a vote. Other maps have far less asphalt paint than this one (Still open 8).
4. **One passes:** clear with it. **None passes:** show the best two or three, each with a short
   reason. You pick or cancel.

**The arrow board:** on 35 T's, today's point sits typically 0.4 m from it. But it reads 3.6 m off at 3

**New, for curving side roads** (quick trials):

- **Follow the curve:** fit a circle to the side road's middle over the 20 m past its start, and carry
  the curve in, only when the circle is tighter than about 100 m.
  - Lanes only, so any map. Moves 34 onto the arrow board and 42 to within 1 m of it.
  - Gets 27 only partway. Can't help 51 (not curved enough to count) or 66.
  - Moves 23 and 49 about 1 m off boards that today's point already matched.
  - Squares the angle: 42 from 63° to 78°, 35 from 37° to 52°.
- **Paint mouth:** as above.
  - Moves all five the board's way. Within 1.5 m of the board at 27, 34 and 51; well short at 42 and 66.
  - Over all T's it sits further from the board than today's point does (0.9 m against 0.4 m).
  - Paved yards break it (29, 32, 44, 46).
- **Trace in:** follow the side road's asphalt inward. Unstable; needs paint finer than 1 m.
    - Can we fit curves to the edge of the side roads by walking them similar to our field walk, then average to center, same for a dirt road?
- **Edge walk** (your idea, proposal, untried): walk each edge of the side road with the field loop's
  edge-walker, using "on asphalt" (or "on dirt, not field") in place of "on field". Fit a curve to
  each edge where the road is its own width, take the middle, and carry it to the main road's edge.
  - Fitting over 10-20 m smooths out the 1 m paint steps that made "trace in" unstable.
  - Gives the road's width as well, so single-lane roads and tracks get one too.
  - Where the edges flare into the corners marks where the mouth starts.
  - Walking the main road's edges the same way gives the asphalt edge reader (build list 8).
  - Weak spots: a paved yard beside the road (the walk runs off round the yard; fall back to the
    lanes, as decided); dirt edges blur into grass; a field beside a track needs the field layer.
  - **Offline trial, 2026-10-01** (53 labelled T's and Y's, one set of settings, 12 walks each):
    - Found both edges at 44. Fell back at 9: yards (21, 44), paving too wide (29, 32, 40), asphalt
      ends (46), Corner T 31, the tight Y 53, track 4.
    - The plain 1 m paint is enough: the blended read lands within 0.06 m of it.
    - Straight side roads: within about 0.1 m of today's point. Nothing new there.
    - Weak five: 27, 34, 42 and 66 move toward the arrow board and square up (27 71° to 81°, 34
      78° to 89°, 42 63° to 71°, 66 46° to 66°). No help at 51.
    - **The catch:** those gains come from fitting the edges as curves. Fitted straight, 27 and 34
      land further from the board than today. Nothing measured says which is right. At 2 the curve
      lands 5 m from the board.
    - Over all T's against the boards: about the same as today (typically 0.6 m off either way).
  - **Second trial, "joined ends", 2026-10-01:** join the steady road's middle to the middle of the
    flared gap at the main road's edge, instead of carrying a fit in. **Worse** than today and the
    first trial (typically 0.9 m from the boards, 26 within 2 m, against 0.6 m and 28-30). The guess
    just moves into the joining curve (27 lands 0.6, 2.2 or 4.0 m off depending on the curve), and
    uneven corners pull the gap's middle toward the bigger corner. Helps 34, 42, 7 and 35; hurts 51 and 2.
- **Kept circle** (untried): the largest circle on asphalt, kept inside the two roads' strips.

**A fit must move the lines, not just the dot.** The clear cuts by each road's strip; the centre only
sets how far out it works. So moving the centre alone leaves the stubs where they were, and the stubs
decide the turns. At 27 both side stubs end about 3 m off the side road's paved middle. At 66 the only
side stub ends on the gravel shoulder, 9 m short of the track's asphalt. So a fit carries its own lines,
and the strips and stubs follow them (proposal).

**Picking by situation** (proposal). Look for the cause in the paint between the main road's edge and
where the side lanes start:

- Straight side road: keep today's point.
- Curves past where its lanes start (34, 42): follow the curve.
- Squares up among the forks (27): take the side road's paved middle 8-16 m from the main road's middle
  and carry it straight in. It lands within about 1 m of the board.
- Lanes swing in before the asphalt (66): paint only.
- Main road bends at the mouth (51, maybe): fix the main road's reading.
- Never offer follow the curve where it can't help.

Our first plan (follow the curve, checked against the paint mouth) fixed only 34 and most of 42, asked
at 6 junctions and missed 51. Y merges, Corner T's and crossroads have no new method yet (Still open 7).

**Stubs:**

- **Today:** each side lane runs on until it enters the other road's strip. Typically 0.5 m short of the
  asphalt edge; 38 of 93 stop over 0.75 m short.
- **Edge from paint** (proposal): the main road's middle line plus its asphalt half-width, read 15-30 m
  either side of the mouth. Today's rule where there is no paint or a yard.
- **Main-road lanes** with through roads off, and lanes at a crossroads: the same rule. Later.
- **Then the builder trims** each stub back to where its turn starts. It can't lengthen one.

## The grade

All proposal. **Fit checks** run on the preview, before anything is deleted, so the tool can choose or
ask. **Clean-up checks** run after the clear. They can't help choose a fit: the preview doesn't yet say
what it would delete. What happens when one fails: Still open 9.

Rules for every check:

- Only paint, lanes and ground height may pass or fail a fit. Signs, map groups, painted masks and the
  host's drive-cost map are votes: they rank fits and add a note.
- A check may stop a fit passing only once it has been shown, offline, to tell good fits from bad.
  Until then it is a vote.
- Every fit needs at least one check that doesn't share its source. Today the paint mouth has none.

| Check | What it checks |
|---|---|
| 1. Middle empty (clean-up) | Nothing inside joins one road's lane to another's; no teal dots. Fails at a crossroads with through roads kept until "nothing joins them" is built. |
| 2. Nothing else touched (clean-up) | Compare before and after, and match it against the clear's own record: anything in one but not the other fails. The respacing (up to 50 m out) has its own limit. Kept through roads still drive end to end; every stub keeps its direction. |
| 3. Stubs in their own road | Each stub ends on asphalt, inside its own road's paving. |
| 4. Stubs reach the edge, level | Each stub ends near the other road's asphalt edge, read at that spot. Both lanes of a road end close together. |
| 5. Stub direction | The last 6-8 m of each stub against its own road's paving just outside the mouth, where it is the road's own width. |
| 6. Centre on the side road's middle | Where the side road's paved middle meets the main road's. |
| 7. Same answer from anywhere | Fit again from 2-3 nearby picks: moves under 0.5 m. |
| Road reading (once per junction) | The lanes' middle line against the paint's. Every fit sits on the lanes' line, so this can't tell fits apart; it flags a junction whose lanes sit off the paint. At a crossroads, both roads. Assumes Still open 1 a). |

**What the trials found:**

- **The road reading works on the main road:** within 1 m at 37 of 48 T's and Y's.
- **No side-road reader is ready for check 6.** One version can't read 34 or 66, and disagrees with
  itself at 27, 42 and 51. Another passes today's point at 27, 34 and 42, and fails 14 sites you never
  flagged. One reader must be chosen offline first, against the arrow boards and your weak list.
- **Asking would not be rare.** Under the first plan, about 15 of 50 T's and Y's would ask before check
  6 even counts. If check 6 asked whenever its two readings were over 2 m apart, about two thirds would.
  That can't meet "ask rarely".

**Shown, not graded:** obstacles in the overlap and the first 10 m of each stub, by type. The clear
doesn't move for them; the list goes to the builder. Room for the builder: Still open 10.

## Split junctions: clearing one part (decided 2026-10-03, not built)

Two parts share one main road, for example 39 / 39se / 39nw or 26n / 26s. Part A is the one you clear;
part B is its twin. Three patch rounds on the build-422 findings only moved the problem around, so these
are your decisions to build from:

1. **B's turn inside A's area is left working.** A's clear stops short of where B's turn leaves (or joins)
   the main road, so B's turn can still be driven. A's stub on that side is longer than usual.
2. **Both parts cleared, through roads off:** the road between them stays, as a piece of main road for the
   builder to join to. The check says it is left on purpose.
3. **One part at a time.** No "clear all parts" choice on the card.
4. **The check calls B's turn fine only if it still drives:** it follows the turn end to end; if a vehicle
   can no longer get onto it or off it, that is a problem. The check never relabels or hides a finding.

Already decided before: a lane from one part's side road across the main road into another part's side
road is a turn, cleared even with through roads kept (the green lane at 39).
